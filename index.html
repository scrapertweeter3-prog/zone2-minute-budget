Zone 2 is a minute budget, not a heart rate

Lifters treat cardio as a mood, and Zone 2 gets prescribed like one: ride easy, whatever easy means that day. A protocol starts with a budget instead. 150 minutes a week is the standard public health floor for moderate aerobic work, and a lifter who wants the aerobic base without stealing recovery from the bar splits it 3 x 50 = 150, three sessions of fifty minutes parked on the days the rack stays cold.

The intensity cap is a number a watch can check. A common starting point is 180 minus age, so a 35 year old lifter caps at 180 - 35 = 145 beats a minute. Inside the session the shape is 10 minutes up, 35 minutes of work, 5 minutes down, and the talk test is the pass fail: full sentences yes, singing no. When the sentence falls apart at minute 30 the cap is wrong, drop it 5 beats and run the next one over.

Priced annually, the budget is 150 x 52 = 7,800 minutes, and 7,800 / 60 = 130 hours of easy work a year. That is the dose that builds the base. The travel week cuts to 2 x 50 = 100, two thirds of the prescription, and 100 x 52 = 5,200 minutes a year, 5,200 / 60 = 86.7 hours, still a floor rather than a collapse. What breaks the protocol is not the smaller dose, it is letting a session drift into intervals, because the budget only pays out at conversational effort.

Placement around lifting is the other half of the dose. Zone 2 sits on off days or after easy pull work, never the night before heavy squats, because the entire point of conversational effort is that it does not bid against the barbell for the same recovery. Hard intervals two days out from a max attempt and Zone 2 the same week are different animals; one spends adaptation, one banks it.

The watch cap and the talk test are the whole gadget. Zone 2 that leaves you breathing through your mouth is intervals wearing a calmer playlist, and intervals already have a slot in the week. The budget is boring on purpose. Boring is what compounds across 130 hours.
